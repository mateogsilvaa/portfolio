A village in Soria is getting a biomethane plant. Residents fear the smell; the company promises there won't be any. Our idea: measure it and publish everything.

The proposal

4 parts
01Five stations

Placed around the village, they measure hydrogen sulphide and ammonia every minute, with a wind vane to know where the wind comes from.

02Residents' reports

Anonymous smell reports, cross-checked with the readings and the wind.

03Fair attribution

An episode only counts against the plant if it exceeds the threshold for 15 minutes or more with the wind blowing from it.

04Data that can't be altered

Each reading is stored so it can't be changed afterwards, and all of them are published on an open website.

Colour-coded alerts

From green to red
Green

Everything below the threshold. Published on the website.

Yellow

A 15-minute attributable exceedance: the company gets an email and has 24 hours to explain the cause.

Orange

No reply within 24 hours or three alerts in a week: the town council and the monitoring committee are notified.

Red

More than 2 hours in a row or a week in orange: a report for the regional government and a notice to residents.

The demo

aireclaro.netlify.app

Two screens with the same data. The residents' one has an illustrated map, the status of each station, the history and a three-step report form.

The town council's one has a real map, alerts, charts, direct contact with the plant and a report ready for the regional government. A test panel lets you trigger an exceedance and watch the whole system respond.
